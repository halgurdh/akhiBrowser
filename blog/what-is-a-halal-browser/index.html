What is a halal browser, and do you need one?

2026-11-08

A halal browser is a web browser that filters what a normal browser leaves to you. It blocks sites and searches for things like adult content, alcohol and gambling, and it keeps unwanted images off your screen.

What a halal browser usually does

  • Blocks sites. Adult, gambling, alcohol and drug sites stop before they load.
  • Forces SafeSearch. Search engines hide explicit results, without you having to set it up.
  • Filters images. Pictures that show nudity are blurred.
  • Blocks ads and trackers. Many unwanted images arrive through advertising, so this helps twice.

What it can't do

No filter is perfect. Some pages get through, and some harmless ones get blocked. A browser also only protects what happens inside that browser: other apps and other browsers on the same device aren't covered. It helps with intention and habit, but it doesn't replace them, or a parent's care.

How to choose one

  • Where does the checking happen? Filtering on your own device means your images don't have to leave it.
  • What leaves your device? Look for a plain list. A browser that says "nothing" should be able to explain how it blocks sites and updates itself.
  • Can you control it? You should be able to see and change every protection.
  • Does it run on your devices? Check before you commit, especially on an iPhone or a Mac.

Halal browser, browser extension or DNS filter?

There are three common ways to filter the web, and they work best together:

  • A halal browser filters inside the browser itself: sites, searches, images and video. Its protections are on from the first page and can be locked with a PIN.
  • A browser extension adds filtering to a browser you already use, such as Chrome. It's quick to try, but it can usually be removed or switched off, and it can't change how the browser itself works.
  • A DNS or router filter blocks whole sites for every app on a device or network. It can't look inside a page, so it can't blur an image or remove music from a video.

For a family, a common setup is a family DNS on the router plus a halal browser on each device, with other browsers removed or blocked. Read how to block haram content on your phone and computer for the full setup.

A checklist for choosing a halal browser

  • Are adult, gambling, alcohol and drug sites blocked from the first page, without setup?
  • Is SafeSearch forced on every search engine, including YouTube?
  • Are images checked on your device, or sent to a server?
  • Can a parent lock the settings with a PIN?
  • Does it say plainly what leaves your device?
  • Is it still updated? A browser that stops getting updates also stops getting security fixes. SalamWeb, one of the first halal browsers, was discontinued in 2021.

Frequently asked questions

Is a halal browser only for Muslims?

No. Anyone who wants fewer ads, no adult content and more control over images can use one. The name describes the values it's built around.

Is a halal browser free?

Many are. AkhiBrowser's protections are free, with optional Premium for extra image controls and parent tools.

Does a halal browser replace parental controls?

It covers the browser. For apps and other browsers, combine it with the controls of the device, such as Google Family Link or Microsoft Family Safety. See a simple parental-controls setup.

Can a halal browser remove music from videos?

Some can. AkhiBrowser removes background music on your device, so the voices stay and the music goes.
