Why prayer times differ between apps and mosques

2026-11-08

Two apps can show different times for the same prayer in the same city. Usually neither is broken: they use a different calculation method.

What stays the same, and what changes

Dhuhr and Maghrib follow the sun's position, so methods mostly agree on them. Fajr and Isha depend on twilight, when the sun is a certain angle below the horizon, and methods choose different angles. Asr also differs by school: the Hanafi time is later than the standard one.

A real example

These are the times for Almere, the Netherlands, on 8 November 2026, from the public Aladhan calculation service:

MethodFajrIsha
Morocco05:4318:48
Muslim World League05:5018:48
Karachi05:5018:55
Moonsighting Committee06:0918:26
ISNA06:1018:35
France (UOIF)06:3018:15

That's 47 minutes between the earliest and latest Fajr. Far from the equator the gaps change with the seasons, and in summer true twilight may not happen at all, so methods also need rules for high latitudes.

Which one should you use?

For a question of practice, ask your local mosque or a scholar you trust, and follow what your community uses. An app can only do the sums.
